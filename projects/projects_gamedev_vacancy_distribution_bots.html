GameDev Vacancy Distribution Bots — Job Aggregator with Moderation and Paid Personal Feeds

A Telegram service that collects game-industry vacancies from 14 public channels in real time, cleans and deduplicates them, publishes curated posts to a 5,300+ subscriber Telegram channel and a 5,500+ member VK community, and delivers keyword-filtered vacancies to paying subscribers through a private bot.

GameDev job postings in Telegram are scattered across dozens of channels, mixed with résumés, course ads, giveaways and cross-promotion. Following all of them by hand is noisy, and the same vacancy is often reposted in several places.

I built a four-process system around that problem. A Telethon userbot listens to the source channels and turns every new post into a clean queue item. A public publisher bot sends each item to an administrator for one-tap moderation and then posts it to the @works_gamedev channel with voting buttons. A private bot sells access through Telegram Stars and lets each subscriber define include and exclude keywords, while a separate private publisher delivers only the matching vacancies to their direct messages.

In a later hardening pass I moved all secrets to environment variables, made publishing and payments idempotent, and replaced fragile text-cleaning logic that could corrupt usernames and links inside vacancies.

The curated stream feeds an audience of more than 10,000 people: 5,300+ subscribers of the Telegram channel and 5,500+ members of the VK community Работа Вакансии GameDev, both filled by this parser.

Outcome: one ingestion pipeline feeding two independent products — a moderated public channel and a monetized personal vacancy feed — without duplicate posts, double-charged subscriptions or queues blocked by inactive users.

One ingestion module feeds two file-based queues. Each queue is consumed by its own independent publisher, while subscriptions, filters and payments live in PostgreSQL.

Two Delivery Channels

Every vacancy accepted by the parser is written to two independent queues. The same content therefore reaches two different audiences, each with its own delivery rules, pace and failure handling.

1Public Channels · 10,800+ Readers

  • The publisher bot checks its queue every 30 minutes and sends up to five new items to the administrator
  • Each item arrives with ✅ Publish and ❌ Reject buttons
  • Approved vacancies are posted to the channel with 👍 / 👎 voting, one vote per user
  • Posts also carry links for adding a résumé and opening a candidate database
  • The same curated vacancies fill the 5,500+ member VK community Работа Вакансии GameDev
  • Unmoderated items older than seven days are cleaned up automatically

2Private Personal Feed

  • Users start a one-time 14-day trial or pay with Telegram Stars
  • Each subscriber sets include keywords (e.g. unity, 3d artist, qa) and exclude keywords (e.g. casino, nft, web3)
  • The private publisher checks its queue every five minutes and sends matching vacancies straight to DMs
  • No include keywords means “send me everything”, minus the excluded words
  • A status screen shows trial, paid period, access end date and auto-renewal state

Overview

The system is built as a pipeline of small, independent processes connected through files and a database rather than one large bot. The parser does not know how vacancies will be published, and the publishers do not know where vacancies came from. This makes it possible to restart, change or pause one part without touching the others.

Ingestion runs on a Telegram user account through Telethon, because bots cannot read arbitrary public channels. On start-up the parser resolves each configured channel, skips the ones that no longer exist, and subscribes to new messages from the rest. Only posts published on the current day (Moscow time) are processed, so a reconnect does not flood the queues with old vacancies.

Before a post is queued it goes through several filters: high-confidence markers of non-vacancy content (résumés, “looking for a job”, giveaways, free courses, game jams), removal of known cross-promotion lines from source channels, a minimum-length check, and fuzzy duplicate detection against the last 300 accepted posts. Photos are downloaded once and copied into each queue so the two publishers can delete their own copies independently.

The private side turns the same stream into a paid product. Access is a combination of a one-time trial and paid periods; payments are made in Telegram Stars, either as a single month or as a native Telegram auto-renewing subscription that the user can cancel from the bot menu.

Queue items are plain JSON files named by timestamp, source channel id and message id. They carry the cleaned text, an optional photo path and source metadata. Every write goes to a temporary file first and is then moved into place with os.replace, so a crash in the middle of a write never leaves a half-written item for a publisher to read.

The public publisher treats a queue file as a small state machine. A _review_sent flag prevents the same item from being sent to the administrator twice. After the channel post succeeds, the returned Telegram message id is written into the file before anything else happens — attaching the vote keyboard, saving votes, deleting the photo. If any of those secondary steps fail, the next run sees the stored message id and only finishes the cleanup instead of posting the vacancy again.

The private publisher loads the list of active recipients from PostgreSQL once per run and walks through the queue. Each item keeps a delivered_to list. Recipients whose filters do not match are marked as handled without a message; recipients who blocked the bot are marked inactive in the database. An item is deleted only when every current recipient has been handled, and partially delivered items are saved back so the next run continues where the previous one stopped.

Access is computed as the later of trial_until and paid_until. A new payment extends access from whichever of the two ends later, so paying during a trial adds the month on top of the remaining trial days. Every payment is first checked against the expected payload, currency (XTR) and amount, then recorded in a payment_receipts table keyed by Telegram's charge id inside a transaction with a row lock on the user.

Telegram's limits are handled explicitly in both publishers. If a photo caption is too long, the vacancy is sent as a text message; if the text itself is too long, it is trimmed to a safe length with a visible marker; if the photo cannot be sent, the post falls back to text only. Raw URLs in vacancies are converted into safe HTML links labeled “ссылка”, with all other text HTML-escaped.

Architecture / Workflow

  • Step 1: The Telethon client resolves the configured job channels and subscribes to their new messages.
  • Step 2: A new post is checked for today's date, its hidden links are restored, and Markdown is converted to plain text.
  • Step 3: Posts with non-vacancy markers, empty text or two lines or fewer are skipped; known promo lines are removed.
  • Step 4: The cleaned text gets the channel signature and is compared with recent posts for fuzzy duplicates.
  • Step 5: The vacancy is written atomically to both queues, with a separate copy of the photo for each.
  • Step 6: The public publisher sends new items to the administrator; approved ones are posted to @works_gamedev with voting buttons.
  • Step 7: The private publisher matches each item against every active subscriber's keywords and sends it to their DMs.
  • Step 8: Users manage trial, payment, auto-renewal and filters through the private bot; access data lives in PostgreSQL.

Challenges & Solutions

  • Challenge: Removing source-channel ads with global text.replace() on short fragments such as “@for” sometimes cut pieces out of usernames, e-mails and application links inside real vacancies.
    Solution: Replaced substring deletion with line-level rules: only whole lines that start with a known promo phrase, or promo lines that contain a known channel URL, are dropped.
    Result: Contact details and application links in vacancies stay intact.
  • Challenge: Many vacancies hide the application link behind text like “Apply here”. Reading only the visible text lost the URL completely.
    Solution: Rebuilt Telegram text-url entities into Markdown and converted them to “label: url” before queuing.
    Result: Every forwarded vacancy keeps a working link to apply.
  • Challenge: A period or closing bracket right after a URL was captured as part of the link, producing broken addresses.
    Solution: Split trailing punctuation from URLs, keeping closing brackets only when they are balanced inside the URL itself; covered the cases with tests.
    Result: Links such as …/job. and …/a_(b) are both rendered correctly.
  • Challenge: If attaching the vote keyboard failed after a successful channel post, a retry published the same vacancy again.
    Solution: Persist the Telegram message id in the queue file immediately after sending and treat all later steps as resumable cleanup.
    Result: Publishing is idempotent; a failed run never creates a duplicate post.
  • Challenge: Telegram can deliver the same successful_payment update more than once, which could extend a subscription twice.
    Solution: Validate payload, currency and amount, and record each charge id in a receipts table with ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING inside a transaction.
    Result: Each payment extends access exactly once.
  • Challenge: A subscriber who blocked the bot never received the message, so the queue item was never considered delivered and stayed in the queue forever.
    Solution: Treat a blocked chat as handled and mark the user inactive in the database.
    Result: The private queue drains normally and inactive users no longer cost a request on every run.
  • Challenge: The “trial expired” notice was sent again to the same users every time the script ran.
    Solution: Added an expired_notice_sent_at column, set only after a successful send and reset when a trial starts.
    Result: Each user gets the offer once; the script is safe to rerun.
  • Challenge: Every queued post ends with the same channel footer. Fuzzy deduplication compared full texts, so two unrelated short vacancies scored around 90 — exactly the duplicate threshold — and a real vacancy could be dropped. After a restart, cached posts (stored on one line) also scored lower against fresh ones.
    Solution: Deduplication now compares only the vacancy body, with the footer removed and whitespace normalized.
    Result: In a test pair of different short vacancies the score fell from 90 to 22, while true reposts still match after a restart.
  • Challenge: Private keyword filters were matched against the post including the footer, so an include word like “геймдев” matched every vacancy and an exclude word like “канал” silently blocked all of them.
    Solution: Moved the footer into a shared constant and matched filters against the vacancy text only.
    Result: Subscribers receive exactly what their keywords describe.
  • Challenge: The private publisher sent messages to all subscribers back to back with no flood-control handling, and an item could be retried forever for a user who kept failing for reasons other than blocking the bot.
    Solution: Added a small delay between sends, a wait-and-retry on Telegram's RetryAfter, and expiry of undeliverable items after three days.
    Result: Delivery stays within Telegram's rate limits and the private queue cannot grow without bound.
  • Challenge: Tokens and IDs were hard-coded, runtime files were created relative to the current directory, and the publisher used a Linux-only lock that crashed on Windows.
    Solution: Moved configuration to .env, anchored all runtime paths under data/, and made the process lock optional where fcntl is unavailable.
    Result: The repository contains no secrets and every component starts the same way on Windows and Linux.

Result

The project turned a noisy set of public job channels into two curated products: a moderated public channel for the GameDev community and a paid personal feed that delivers only the vacancies a subscriber actually cares about.

Both products are fed by the same ingestion pipeline, but each publisher can fail, restart or be changed independently. The hardening pass made the system safe to rerun: no duplicate posts, no double-counted payments, no repeated notices, and no queues held up by users who left.

Today the pipeline feeds a combined audience of more than 10,800 people across Telegram and VK, in addition to the paid personal feed.

From an engineering perspective, the project shows end-to-end ownership of a small Telegram product: userbot ingestion, text processing under messy real-world input, file-based queues with idempotent consumers, a relational access model, and native in-app payments.
